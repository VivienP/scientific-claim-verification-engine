# Post-sprint investigation: lactate benchmark failure attribution

> Implementation follow-up, 2026-05-07: this investigation was written against
> the original 1/25 saved benchmark run. The current branch has since landed
> bibliography-aware resolution, exact DOI lookup, PubMed fallback, and verifier
> rubric changes. The measured end-to-end result is now 7/25 agreement
> (macro-F1 0.28, 17/20 audited DOI matches) in
> `eval/e2e/reference_paper_v1_results.md`. The core launch conclusion did not
> change: 7/25 is still below launch quality, and the dominant remaining
> blockers are evidence delivery, multi-source aggregation, and partial-support
> calibration rather than generic resolver scoring alone.

## Executive summary
The previous 1/25 agreement number is correct when recomputed from the saved benchmark artifacts, but the prior diagnosis was too narrow: resolver failure is the first blocker for most claims, yet only 8 of the 24 disagreements look fully recoverable after the primary blocker is fixed. The most surprising finding is benchmark-integrity related: `lactate_review_003` names Goodwin 2007, but the benchmark DOI field `10.1007/BF00382568` resolves to an unrelated geology article; the actual Goodwin article is `10.1177/193229680700100414` according to the source bibliography and external bibliographic pages. The highest-leverage fix is not a generic OpenAlex scorer tweak; it is bibliography-aware citation resolution that preserves citation numbers/ranges, extracts titles/DOIs/PMIDs from the reference list, and routes PMID/PMC sources into PubMed/PMC retrieval. Launch should be delayed: the current system is not failing only because it picks the wrong DOI, it also lacks the evidence-delivery and verifier-rubric behavior needed for unsupported-by-absence and partially-supported multi-source claims.

Evidence base: saved run artifacts, source text, local code inspection, local SQLite cache inspection, and web spot-checks. New Anthropic spend in this investigation was `$0.00`; the platform rejected a direct API verifier probe, so verifier-on-oracle results below are static/source-backed rather than live LLM reruns.

## Stage-by-stage investigation findings

### Extraction
What I measured: compared the saved extracted claim fields in `reference_paper_v1_pipeline_output.json` against the manuscript source text and bibliography markers recorded in `bibliography_probe.json`. I also checked the extraction prompt and resolver preconditions.

What I found: extraction preserved the 25 claim texts well enough for verdict scoring; I did not find a broad claim-text-mangling problem. The material extraction failure is citation structure: the extractor emits author lists and sometimes years, but it does not preserve the actual bracket markers such as `[81-83]`, `[99,100]`, or `[70-73]`. All 7 claims with `cited_year=null` are multi-citation or citation-range claims in the source text, and all 7 are unresolved because `resolve_citations()` skips any claim with `cited_year is None`. This confirms the previous sprint pattern, but the cause is not simply "year missing"; it is loss of citation-number/bibliography linkage.

Code references: `src/extract.py:38-39` asks for cited authors/year, `src/extract.py:55-56` says not to hallucinate citations, and `src/resolve.py:52` short-circuits yearless claims. Previous diagnosis: partially correct. It identified the null-year symptom but not the structural need to preserve numbered citation anchors and reference-list entries.

### Resolution
What I measured: reconstructed the current query for each claim and compared saved selected DOI/title/scores against the benchmark identifier and source bibliography. I inspected the resolver and OpenAlex scorer implementation.

What I found: resolution is still the largest first blocker. Among 24 disagreements, 14 first fail on wrong source resolution and 7 on yearless citation-context loss. But the existing `similarity_score` is not semantic similarity: `src/clients/openalex.py:55` computes it from publication-year distance, so a wrong paper in the same year can score 1.0. The best-result picker (`src/clients/openalex.py:90`) combines that year score with token overlap, which explains why generic claim text can beat the cited paper. `title_match_score` is also not a sufficient gate: it flagged some bad papers, but it also flagged the one true positive (`lactate_review_023`, title_match_score 0.125) as low confidence.

A second, more serious finding: benchmark DOI fields are not all reliable. `lactate_review_003` expects Goodwin 2007, but the benchmark DOI is `10.1007/BF00382568`; web lookup shows that DOI is a Contributions to Mineralogy and Petrology paper, while the source bibliography and SAGE/WashU pages identify Goodwin as DOI `10.1177/193229680700100414`. This means DOI-accuracy calculations using that field overstate or misclassify at least one resolver error.

Code references: `src/resolve.py:26` builds queries from first three authors, optional year, and first five claim words; `src/resolve.py:52` skips missing years; `src/clients/openalex.py:55`, `:76`, `:90`, and `:153` implement year score, lexical overlap, candidate selection, and low-confidence flagging. Previous diagnosis: correct that resolver is dominant, wrong that a simple author/year/title scoring fix is enough.

### Full-text retrieval
What I measured: classified every disagreement by whether the saved run had no source, wrong source, right DOI with no full text/abstract, or full-text passages. I also checked source availability for the spot-check cases and recoverable claims.

What I found: retrieval is a second-order blocker that makes resolver gain smaller than it appears. Claims 010 and 016 already resolved to the correct DOI but still returned `not_addressed` because CrossRef metadata had no abstract and the current retrieval chain did not fetch PubMed abstracts or publisher full text. Several recoverable-looking resolver cases are only recoverable if the next sprint adds PMID/PMC/PubMed metadata handling: Bonaventura has PMCID `PMC4306774`, Kim 2016 has PMCID `PMC5069392`, and Birklein/Jansson have PubMed abstracts that the current CrossRef path did not ingest. Old proceedings, Wiley, IEEE, and non-OA sports-science papers remain hard blockers even with a perfect resolver.

Code references: `src/fetch_fulltext.py:18-29` tries OA URL, PMC, Unpaywall PDF, then abstract fallback. It does not query PubMed by PMID/title and it does not use source titles as evidence. Previous diagnosis: incomplete. Wrong DOI is often first, but correct DOI is not sufficient when full text or abstracts are missing.

### BM25 passage selection
What I measured: reviewed saved `retrieval_status`, `n_passages_used`, and source titles; inspected BM25 selection behavior. I could not rerun `select_passages()` because the project dependency install was interrupted and the bundled Python lacks `rank_bm25` and `tiktoken`.

What I found: BM25 is not the dominant measured first blocker in the saved run because most selected passages came from wrong papers. But it remains a credible next blocker for full-text recoveries: the chunker creates one chunk per detected IMRAD section, and BM25 sees only claim text, not citation title, reference title, or verifier rationale. For short/general claims, the top passage can be a broad abstract or introduction rather than the exact evidence sentence. The current artifacts do not prove BM25 is broken; they prove the current benchmark cannot estimate BM25 quality until resolver and retrieval are fixed enough to feed the right papers.

Code references: `src/bm25_selector.py:72` selects top-k chunks by token overlap/BM25; `src/bm25_selector.py:102-110` returns empty on no overlap or zero max score; `src/chunker.py:51` produces section-aware chunks but not paragraph/window chunks. Previous diagnosis: unverified. The prior sprint treated verifier honesty as established from wrong-source cases, but did not isolate BM25 on correct sources.

### Verifier
What I measured: inspected the verifier prompts, the saved verdict distribution, SciFact evaluation mapping, and the direct pipeline output. The direct Anthropic oracle-passage probe could not be executed because the platform rejected the network/API escalation; see `oracle_verifier_results.json`.

What I found: the verifier is not merely "honest"; it is conservatively abstention-biased by design. Both abstract and full-text prompts say to err toward `not_addressed` when evidence is short, general, insufficient, or off-topic. That is desirable for wrong papers but harmful for partially supported and unsupported-by-absence claims. There is no parser collapse of `partially_supported`: the schema and unit tests allow it, and the pipeline emitted it once. The collapse is evidence/rubric driven. SciFact F1=0.94 does not validate this behavior because `scripts/eval_scifact.py:102` is verifier-only and `scripts/eval_scifact.py:184` maps `partially_supported` to `supported`.

Code references: `src/verify.py:37-41` and `src/verify.py:138-142` define partial support but also push uncertainty to `not_addressed`; `src/verify.py:357` short-circuits abstract verification when source abstract is missing. Previous diagnosis: partially wrong. The verifier is honest on irrelevant passages, but the prompt/rubric will still block unsupported-by-absence and partial multi-source claims after resolver fixes.

## Verifier-on-oracle-passages experiment
The planned live experiment did not complete in this environment. The bundled Python had none of the project dependencies (`anthropic`, `httpx`, `rank_bm25`, `tiktoken`, `pymupdf`, `pytest`, `ruff`), `.venv` creation failed during `ensurepip`, the later dependency install was interrupted, and a direct Anthropic API probe was rejected by the platform approval layer. I therefore did not invent oracle-passage verdicts.

Instead, `eval/e2e/probes/oracle_verifier_results.json` records a static recovery assessment. It marks 8 disagreements as fully recoverable after the primary blocker is fixed: `003`, `006`, `007`, `010`, `011`, `018`, `020`, and `025`. This is the upper bound supported by this run: current agreement 1/25 plus 8 recoverable claims = 9/25, or 36%. The static assessment is intentionally conservative: claims with wrong DOI plus non-OA source access, multi-source aggregation, or unsupported-by-absence rubric issues are not counted as resolver-fix gain.

The next sprint should rerun the live oracle experiment once dependencies are available. Use the current verifier prompt, corrected source identifiers, and manually selected evidence passages for at least the 14 wrong-DOI cases. That result should replace the static ceiling in this report.

## Quantified improvement plan
1. Bibliography-aware citation resolution with DOI/PMID/PMCID extraction. One sentence: parse numbered references and preserve bracket markers from extraction through resolution, then resolve by reference title/identifier before using OpenAlex search. Code locations: `src/extract.py`, `src/resolve.py`, `src/clients/pmc.py`, new small bibliography parser. Cost: 8-12 hours. Estimated gain: 16-24 pp immediately (`006`, `007`, `018`, `020`, plus likely `003` after benchmark DOI correction and `011` with PubMed abstract). Risk: false linkage in multi-citation ranges; regression watch: citation marker precision and no silent source substitution. Dependencies: benchmark DOI correction for claim `003`; PubMed/PMC retrieval for PMID-only entries.

2. PubMed/PMC abstract and full-text fallback. One sentence: when CrossRef/OpenAlex has DOI/title but abstract is null, query PubMed/PMC by DOI, PMID, PMCID, or title before falling back to `not_addressed`. Code locations: `src/fetch_fulltext.py`, `src/clients/pmc.py`, new PubMed client. Cost: 5-8 hours. Estimated gain: 8-16 pp (`010`, `011`, and PMID-only Bonaventura claims after bibliography resolution). Risk: PubMed abstracts are not full text; regression watch: evidence_quality should distinguish PubMed abstract from full-text passage.

3. Replace `similarity_score` semantics and candidate diagnostics. One sentence: stop reporting year proximity as similarity; expose candidate title/author/year/venue/identifier scores and require agreement among multiple signals before `found=True`. Code locations: `src/clients/openalex.py`, `src/resolve.py`, report schema. Cost: 4-6 hours. Estimated gain: mostly precision, not direct agreement; prevents high-confidence wrong papers and converts several wrong DOI cases to low-confidence unresolved. Risk: lower citation_found_rate; regression watch: claim `023` must still resolve despite low current title_match_score.

4. Add unsupported-by-absence / citation-does-not-support rubric. One sentence: separate `not_addressed` from "the cited source addresses the area but does not support this claim" so unsupported claims like `001`, `012`, and `019` can be judged. Code locations: `src/verify.py`, verdict schema/docs/tests. Cost: 6-10 hours. Estimated gain: 4-8 pp after correct sources are delivered; no gain while resolver is wrong. Risk: overcalling unsupported from weak abstracts; regression watch: wrong-paper cases must remain `not_addressed`.

5. Multi-source partial-support aggregation. One sentence: for bracket ranges, verify each cited source separately and aggregate claim-level support/contradiction/absence. Code locations: pipeline orchestration around `resolve_citations`, report aggregation, verifier input shape. Cost: 12-18 hours. Estimated gain: 8-16 pp on partial claims (`004`, `005`, `011`, `017`, `021`) but only after bibliography parsing. Risk: cost and complexity; regression watch: provenance must keep per-source steps.

6. Correct and harden the benchmark identifiers. One sentence: audit every `primary_source_doi`/PMID field against the bibliography before using DOI accuracy as a metric. Code locations: `eval/e2e/reference_paper_v1_verdicts.json`, probe validator only. Cost: 2-4 hours. Estimated gain: 0 pipeline pp, but prevents misleading resolver metrics. Risk: changing benchmark labels after seeing results; regression watch: keep a changelog with source-backed corrections.

## Stop criteria for the next sprint
Do not ship the current launch claim below 15/25 agreement on this benchmark after bibliography and PubMed/PMC fixes. Ship a narrow launch only at >=20/25 agreement and with zero unsupported clinical/numeric claims silently returned as `not_addressed`. Between 15/25 and 19/25, launch only if remaining misses are access-limited old/non-OA papers and the report surfaces low-confidence source resolution clearly. Below 15/25 after the top two fixes, reconsider the wedge: cited-claim verification over bibliography-cited literature may be too broad for the current MVP, and the safer wedge would be DOI/PMID-provided verification or reference-list-normalized documents only.

## Surprising findings
- The benchmark itself contains at least one wrong DOI: `10.1007/BF00382568` is not Goodwin 2007. This directly affects resolver-accuracy claims.
- `similarity_score=1.0` means exact year match, not source similarity. The name is misleading and contributed to overconfident diagnosis.
- The one successful claim, `023`, has `resolution_low_confidence=true` because title-token overlap is low after query construction. A simple low-confidence threshold would have hidden the only success.
- The extractor did better than expected on claim text but worse than needed on citation structure. The missing artifact is the bracket marker, not just author/year.
- The verifier is not proven sound by wrong-source abstentions. Its current rubric makes several human `unsupported` labels unrecoverable because absence of support becomes `not_addressed`.
- The real-tool benchmark and lactate benchmark share a high `not_addressed` rate, but not necessarily the same root cause. Lactate exposes numbered-reference/bibliography failure; the real-tool reports also include low source availability and tool-output citation quality issues.
